# How do plants respond to stimuli without a nervous system?

Plants respond to stimuli via chemical means, through hormones and changes in cell water content, leading to movement or growth.

Use the mnemonics below to understand how plants respond to stimuli.

:::tip[Mnemonic 1]

**"Plants Hug Hormones, Water Content Moves Gracefully."**

:::

**Explanation:**  
This mnemonic captures key elements of the answer. "Plants Hug" reminds you that plants rely on chemical mechanisms. "Hormones" represents the role of chemicals in response. "Water Content" signals the changes in cell water content, and "Moves Gracefully" reflects the resulting movement or growth. Together, this sentence helps recall that plants respond to stimuli through hormones and changes in water content.

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 2]

**"Chemical Hormones and Water Change Make Plants Sway."**

:::

**Explanation:**  
The mnemonic reminds us that plants respond to stimuli using "Chemical" means, mainly through "Hormones" and "Water Change," which causes them to "Make" movements or "Sway" through growth without a nervous system.

---

**"Plants Don't Need Nerves, Just Hormones and Water!"**

:::

**Explanation:**  
This mnemonic captures the heart of the answer by emphasizing that plants rely on "Hormones and Water," which serve as the chemical means and changes in cell water content necessary for their responses to stimuli. This catchy phrase is easy to remember and highlights the key components.

This encapsulates the exact wording of the answer.
