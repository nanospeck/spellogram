### Important Questions and Answers

#### 1. What criteria do we use to decide whether something is alive?
A: We look for evidence of movement (visible or molecular), and the ability to repair and maintain structures.

#### 2. Why are molecular movements necessary for life?
A: Molecular movements are necessary to repair and maintain the ordered structure of living organisms which is important to keep them alive.

#### 3. What is meant by life processes and why are they important?
A: Life processes are the maintenance functions that include nutrition, respiration, transport, and excretion, essential to prevent damage and breakdown in organisms.

#### 4. How do autotrophs obtain their food?
A: Autotrophs, like green plants, obtain food by photosynthesis using inorganic materials like carbon dioxide and water with the help of sunlight and chlorophyll.

#### 5. What are the requirements and products of photosynthesis?
A: Requirements: Sunlight, chlorophyll, carbon dioxide, and water. Products: Carbohydrates and oxygen.

#### 6. Describe the process of photosynthesis.
A: Photosynthesis involves absorption of light by chlorophyll, conversion of light energy to chemical energy, and the reduction of carbon dioxide into carbohydrates.

#### 7. How do heterotrophs obtain nutrition?
A: Heterotrophs rely on complex substances derived from autotrophs or other organisms, which are broken down into simpler substances inside their bodies.

#### 8. What role does saliva play in digestion?
A: Saliva contains the enzyme salivary amylase which breaks down starch into simpler sugars, aiding in initial digestion in the mouth.

#### 9. How is food transported in the human body?
A: Food is transported from the digestive system to body cells via the bloodstream, where digested nutrients are absorbed and circulated.

#### 10. Differentiate between aerobic and anaerobic respiration.
A: Aerobic respiration uses oxygen to fully break down glucose into carbon dioxide and water, releasing more energy, while anaerobic respiration happens without oxygen, producing lactic acid or ethanol and energy.

#### 11. How is oxygen transported in human beings?
A: Oxygen is transported through the body bound to hemoglobin in red blood cells after being absorbed in the lungs from inhaled air.

#### 12. What is the structure and function of alveoli?
A: Alveoli are tiny air sacs in the lungs with thin walls rich in blood vessels, designed to maximize the exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide.

#### 13. What is double circulation and why is it necessary?
A: Double circulation involves the separate circulation of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood, which ensures efficient oxygen supply to organs and energy regulation in warm-blooded animals.

#### 14. Describe the transportation functions of xylem and phloem in plants.
A: Xylem transports water and minerals from roots to leaves, whereas phloem distributes sugars and other metabolic products throughout the plant.

#### 15. How do plants excrete waste products?
A: Plants excrete waste through processes such as transpiration, passage into soil, or storage in leaves and old xylem.

#### 16. How do human kidneys function in excretion?
A: Human kidneys filter blood to remove wastes like urea, reabsorbing required substances and forming urine which is expelled via the urinary system.

#### 17. What is the role of guard cells in plants?
A: Guard cells regulate the opening and closing of stomata, controlling gas exchange and transpiration in plants.

#### 18. What is transpiration and its significance in plants?
A: Transpiration is the loss of water vapor from plant aerial parts, mainly leaves, aiding in nutrient transport and temperature regulation.

#### 19. How does the human circulatory system handle blood pressure?
A: The circulatory system ensures regulated blood pressure through elastic and muscular arterial walls, maintaining efficient blood flow and minimizing rupture risks.

#### 20. What are the methods plants use to manage excretory substances and energy needs?
A: Plants manage waste via vacuolar storage, excretion into the soil, or other metabolic adaptations to balance energy needs effectively.
