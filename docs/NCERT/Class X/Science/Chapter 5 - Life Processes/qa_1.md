# 1. What criteria do we use to decide whether something is alive?

## Answer:
A: We look for evidence of movement (visible or molecular), and the ability to repair and maintain structures.

Saliva is essential for starting digestion by breaking down starch into simpler sugars right in your mouth. Use the mnemonics below to understand its roles.

:::tip[Mnemonic 1]

**M.O.R.E. Life!**

:::

**Explanation:**
This mnemonic helps remember the key criteria that determine if something is alive:

- **M**: Movement (evidence of)
- **O**: Operation (ability to maintain)
- **R**: Repair (ability to repair)
- **E**: Existence (of living structures)

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 2]

**Moms Make Repairs**

:::

- **M** for **Movement**
- **M**ake (**Molecular** movement included)
- **R**epairs (**Repair and maintain structures**)

**Explanation:** This mnemonic links mothers to the essential criteria for life by associating their constant motion and ability to fix things with the ability of living organisms to move and repair themselves.

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 3]

**Move To Fix - Eyes Blink, Cells Mend**

:::

**Explanation:**
- **Move**: Represents movement (either visible or molecular).
- **Fix**: Implies the ability to repair.
- **Eyes Blink**: Suggests visible movement.
- **Cells Mend**: Conveys cellular or molecular repair.

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 4]

**Moving Robins Repair Marvelously**

:::

**Explanation:**
- **Moving**: Represents "evidence of movement"
- **Robins**: A visual cue for "visible" movement
- **Repair**: Stands for "ability to repair"
- **Marvelously**: Suggests maintaining structures is done effectively, as living organisms do.

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 5]

**Move & Mend to Befriend Life**

:::

**Explanation:**
This mnemonic simplifies the criteria into the memorable phrase "Move & Mend," highlighting that living entities can move and mend (repair and maintain structures), which are crucial characteristics of life.
