# 9. What is a substitution reaction, and how does it occur in saturated hydrocarbons?

## Answer:

A substitution reaction occurs when an atom or group is replaced by another atom or group. In saturated hydrocarbons, chlorine can replace hydrogen in the presence of sunlight.

## Mnemonics

:::tip[Mnemonic 1]

**Sunny Chlorine Swaps Hydrogen**

:::

**Mnemonic Explanation:**

- **Sunny**: Represents the presence of sunlight necessary for the reaction.
- **Chlorine**: The atom that replaces hydrogen.
- **Swaps**: Indicates the substitution process.
- **Hydrogen**: The atom being replaced.

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 2]

**Saturated Chlorine Sunshine Swap**

:::

**Mnemonic Explanation:**

- **Saturated**: Relates to saturated hydrocarbons where the reaction occurs.
- **Chlorine**: Performs the substitution.
- **Sunshine**: Necessary condition of sunlight for the reaction.
- **Swap**: Refers to the substitution action.

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 3]

**Sunny Chlorine Swap: Hydrogen Out!**

:::

**Mnemonic Explanation:**

- **Sunny**: Indicates the need for sunlight in the reaction.
- **Chlorine Swap**: Chlorine replaces hydrogen.
- **Hydrogen Out!**: Represents the substitution of hydrogen atoms.

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 4]

**Sunlit Chlorine Swaps Hydrogen in Sat.**

:::

**Mnemonic Explanation:**

- **Sunlit**: Reminds of the sunlight's presence.
- **Chlorine**: Involves chlorine in the reaction.
- **Swaps**: Process of replacing.
- **Hydrogen in Sat**: In saturated hydrocarbons, hydrogen is replaced.

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 5]

**Substitute Chlorine Hydrogens Under Sun**

:::

**Mnemonic Explanation:**

- **Substitute**: Refers to the substitution reaction.
- **Chlorine**: The atom doing the replacing.
- **Hydrogens**: What is replaced.
- **Under Sun**: Refers to the presence of sunlight needed for the reaction.
