# 6. How are saturated and unsaturated carbon compounds different?

Saturated compounds contain only single bonds between carbon atoms, while unsaturated compounds have one or more double or triple bonds. Use the mnemonics below to understand their differences.

:::tip[Mnemonic 1]

**Saturated Soldiers March, Unsaturated Unicorns Dance & Triple-flip**

:::

**Explanation:**

- "Saturated Soldiers March": Saturated compounds have only single bonds, implying a regular and 'single-file' structure like marching soldiers.
- "Unsaturated Unicorns Dance & Triple-flip": Unsaturated compounds have flexibility with double or triple bonds, allowing for 'dance and flipping' movements.

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 2]

**Sara's Single Surprise, Uncle's Double Trouble**

:::

**Explanation:**

- "Sara's": Stands for "Saturated" compounds with "Single bonds."
- "Surprise": Implies simplicity and predictability, like single bonds.
- "Uncle's": Represents "Unsaturated" compounds.
- "Double Trouble": Implies complexity, referring to double or triple bonds.

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 3]

**Saturated Singles Unite, Unsaturated Double-Triple Dates!**

:::

**Explanation:**

- **Saturated** compounds contain **single bonds**, represented as "singles."
- **Unsaturated** compounds have **double or triple bonds**, depicted as "Double-Triple Dates."

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 4]

**Single-Bonded Saturation, Double Trouble Unsaturation**

:::

**Explanation:**

- "Single-Bonded Saturation": Helps remember that saturated compounds have only single bonds.
- "Double Trouble Unsaturation": Highlights the presence of double or triple bonds in unsaturated compounds, with "trouble" implying added complexity.

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 5]

**Saturated Sings, Unsaturated Duets or Trios**

:::

**Explanation:**

- "Saturated Sings": Reminds us that saturated compounds have only single bonds.
- "Unsaturated Duets or Trios": Indicates that unsaturated compounds have double or triple bonds.
