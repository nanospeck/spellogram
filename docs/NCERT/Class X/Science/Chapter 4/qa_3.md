# 3. Describe how a molecule of hydrogen is formed through covalent bonding.

## Answer:
Two hydrogen atoms share their electrons to form a molecule, H2, achieving a helium-like electronic configuration of two electrons in their outer shell. Use the mnemonics below to understand this process.

:::tip[Mnemonic 1]
**H2 Heals by Sharing: Two Atoms, Two Electrons, Perfect Pairing!**

:::

**Explanation:**  
This mnemonic emphasizes the sharing aspect of covalent bonding, where two hydrogen atoms share their electrons to achieve a stable configuration, akin to helium's electron arrangement of two electrons in their outer shell.

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 2]
**Two Happy Hydrogen Friends Share to Be Helium Happier**

:::

**Explanation:**  
- **Two Happy Hydrogen Friends**: Represents the two hydrogen atoms involved.
- **Share**: Denotes the sharing of electrons.
- **to Be Helium Happier**: Indicates achieving a stable electronic configuration like helium.

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 3]
**Hydrogen Harmony: Sharing E-Tunes, Helium Soon!**

:::

**Explanation:**  
This mnemonic highlights the harmony involved when hydrogen atoms share electrons ("E-Tunes") to reach a stable state similar to helium, achieving a duet just like helium's stable two-electron configuration.

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 4]
**Sharing Halves Makes Helium**

:::

**Explanation:**  
- **Sharing**: Refers to covalent bond sharing of electrons.
- **Halves**: Each hydrogen atom contributes one electron.
- **Makes Helium**: Achieving a stable, helium-like configuration with two electrons.

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 5]
**Happy H2 Twins Hold Hands**

:::

**Explanation:**  
This mnemonic is designed to convey the idea that two hydrogen atoms (“twins”) form a stable pair by sharing electrons (“holding hands”), resembling the noble gas configuration of helium.
