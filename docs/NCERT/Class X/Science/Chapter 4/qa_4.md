# 4. How does a double bond form between two oxygen atoms, and what is its electron dot structure?

### Answer:
Each oxygen atom shares two electrons with another oxygen atom for a total of four shared electrons, forming a double bond.

### Mnemonics:

:::tip[Mnemonic 1]

**"OO: Share Two for Double True."**

:::

**Explanation:**  
The letters "OO" symbolize the two oxygen atoms joining together. "Share Two" reminds you that each oxygen shares two electrons. "Double True" indicates a true double bond is formed through this electron sharing.

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 2]

**"Oxygen Ties Two Shoes, Making Double Knots."**

:::

**Explanation:**  
This visual mnemonic helps remind you that each oxygen atom shares "two" electrons, akin to tying two shoes, resulting in a "double" bond like making double knots.

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 3]

**"Two Elephants Share Four Peanuts for Double Points."**

:::

**Explanation:**  
"Two Elephants" signify the two oxygen atoms, "Share Four Peanuts" indicates the sharing of electrons, and "Double Points" refers to the formation of a double bond.

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 4]

**"Two Oxygen Pairs Form One Strong Double Stair."**

:::

**Explanation:**  
- "Two Oxygen" refers to the two oxygen atoms involved.
- "Pairs" signifies sharing of electrons.
- "Form One" calls attention to making one double bond.
- "Strong Double" highlights the bond type, and "Stair" represents the structured electron dot diagram visually aligning the pairs.

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 5]

**"Double O's Share Two and Two, Forming Bond's View."**

:::

**Explanation:**  
"Double O's" relates to the two oxygen atoms, "Share Two and Two" emphasizes each sharing two electrons, and "Forming Bond's View" helps visualize the formation of a double bond.
