# 1. What is the atomic number of carbon, and how are its electrons distributed in its shells?

The atomic number of carbon is 6, with an electronic configuration of 2, 4, meaning it has 4 valence electrons. Use the mnemonics below to understand these concepts.

:::tip[Mnemonic 1]

**Carbon 6: Two, Four - Valence is galore!**

:::

**Explanation:**  
This mnemonic emphasizes the atomic number (6) followed by the electronic configuration "2, 4", indicating carbon's valence electrons, capturing the key specifics of the answer through a rhyming phrase.

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 2]

**Carbon Six: 2 then 4, valence likes to explore.**

:::

**Explanation:**  
This mnemonic helps recall that carbon's atomic number is 6, with electrons configured as 2 in the first shell and 4 in the valence shell. The phrase "valence likes to explore" hints at carbon having 4 valence electrons.

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 3]

**Six Constellation, Two Stars First, Four Shining.**

:::

**Explanation:**  
This mnemonic helps remember that carbon's atomic number is 6 (Six Constellation), its electron configuration starts with 2 electrons in the first shell (Two Stars First) and 4 electrons in its valence shell (Four Shining).

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 4]

**Carbon's 6 Gear: 2 Foundation, 4 Converse.**

:::

**Explanation:**  
"Carbon's 6" helps you remember the atomic number is 6.  
"Gear" stands for the electronic configuration, which is how electrons are organized.  
"2 Foundation" signifies the first shell having 2 electrons, forming a stable base.  
"4 Converse" points to the 4 valence electrons available for bonding.

---

:::tip[Mnemonic 5]

**Carbon Cafe: 6 Beans, 2 Espresso, 4 Lattés**

:::

**Explanation:**  
"Carbon Cafe" refers to carbon itself.  
"6 Beans" signifies the atomic number of 6.  
"2 Espresso" and "4 Lattés" represent the electronic configuration: 2 electrons in the first shell and 4 valence electrons, respectively.
